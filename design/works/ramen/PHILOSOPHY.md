# Single Field

A movement built on the conviction that one thing, shown whole, says more than many things shown
together. Every surface is a single flat colour taken from a single material, and the material is
named once, in letters large enough to be read from across a room. Nothing is mixed; nothing is
blended into a gradient; the field is the statement.

Space is the field itself. Type sits on it like a stamp on a sack: heavy, square-shouldered, set so
large that its counters become rooms and its strokes become walls. Small text keeps a strict margin
and a strict column, so the giant letters can lean against the edge without the page falling apart.
The rhythm is a series, not a story: the same grid, the same placement, a new colour each time, so
the eye compares and the difference is the content. This sameness is laboured over until each
repetition lands on exactly the same lines, the product of painstaking, master-level adjustment.

Colour is never chosen for mood. It is sampled from the thing: the silver-blue back of a dried fish,
the blue-leaning red of a chilli dried in the sun, the dark green of a leek leaf, the black of a
peppercorn. White and that black do all the remaining work. The photographs obey the same law: the
object is lit by one window, shot straight down, and laid on its own colour so that the paper and the
page become one surface, with only the true shadow left behind, retouched with meticulous care.

Scale carries the hierarchy: one enormous word, one object, one short line, a band of measured
facts, and then quiet. Rules are thick and few. Corners are square. The finished work should feel
like a printed packet front and a poster at once, crafted by someone who has set a thousand of them
and knows that the last millimetre of spacing is what makes the field hold still.
