# Patient Sediment

A visual philosophy for images that are made the way a slow liquid settles: by waiting, by repetition,
by letting weight find its place.

**Time made visible as layers.** Every composition is a record of something that happened slowly. Days,
pours, rests — each becomes one mark, and the marks accumulate in strict order until the whole reads
as a single surface, like strata in a cliff or tide lines on a jar. Nothing is illustrated; it is
measured. The viewer should sense duration before reading a single word, the way one senses age in
a well-used vessel. The work must look as if it were recorded patiently over weeks by a hand of
absolute discipline, every mark set with painstaking attention.

**Vessels as the only form.** The single recurring shape is the rounded square of a heavy glass
container — a superellipse, neither circle nor box. It is drawn in fine lines, repeated at a small
scale until it becomes texture, then occasionally enlarged until it becomes architecture. Liquid
inside each vessel is a flat level with a meniscus hairline; heavier tones always sit lower. Gravity
is the composition's only rule, and it is obeyed with master-level precision: no element floats
unless it is lighter than what lies beneath it.

**Colour from material, never from decoration.** Palettes are taken from what things actually are:
the grey-green of crushed leaves, the amber of grain syrup held to a window, the garnet of dried
berries in spirit, the carbon of ground ink. Each colour appears as a sequence of its own
concentrations, pale to saturated, so a single hue carries a whole timeline. The ground is the
colour of paper that light has passed through, warm or cool by a degree. There are no gradients for
effect — only the meticulously calibrated steps of a substance becoming more itself.

**Space as stillness.** Large fields are left empty so the eye can rest the way a liquid rests. Dense
regions of accumulation are bounded by wide margins, and the eye travels them slowly, left to right,
top to bottom, like reading a ledger. Scale contrast is extreme and deliberate: hundreds of tiny,
identical units against one quiet, generous void. Every alignment is the product of countless
refinements; spacing is a ratio, never a guess.

**Words as instrument labels.** Text is whispered and functional: a numeral at the edge of a scale, a
day count, a single name set in a thin serif, a tiny upright sans for units. Type behaves like the
engraving on laboratory glass — exact, sparse, slightly cold — and it never explains. The image
should feel like a plate from a treatise on an imaginary science of waiting, executed with the
care of someone at the very top of their craft, where the only proof offered is the patience
visible in the marks themselves.
