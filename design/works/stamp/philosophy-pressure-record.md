# Pressure Record

A movement that treats a single pressed mark as evidence. Every impression is a record of one
instant: how hard the hand came down, which way the wrist leaned, how fast the paper was moving
underneath. Nothing about it is decorative; the beauty is in the fact that no two can be the same.

Space is the white of office paper, left mostly empty, ruled by thin printed lines that were never
meant to be looked at. The lines are carbon blue, the colour of a form printed cheaply and a
ballpoint signature, and they set out the boxes into which something must be placed. Red appears
only where a decision was made. One colour for the system, one colour for the act; the grey of a
steel tray is allowed underneath, as the surface everything slides across.

Form comes from carving. The seal's letters are cut as straight, square strokes that run into the
rim the way a knife leaves them, each corner very slightly off true. The impression is never the
seal: it is the seal minus the places the ink did not reach, a broken rim on the side the hand
lifted, a drag where the paper moved, white flecks where the fibre held no ink. These flaws are
generated with painstaking care, so that a field of them reads as a study of many hands rather
than one stamp copied many times.

Rhythm is the conveyor's. Repetition with variation: the same mark, the same box, again and again,
with the record of each attempt left in place. Composition follows the forms themselves: boxes in
a row, a seam between two sheets, a figure struck through twice. Hierarchy is set by colour alone;
red always wins.

Text is a whisper and a scrawl. Printed labels are small and plain; anything written by a person
is a quick marker hand, a word or two at most. The work should look meticulously made by someone

level attention spent on the smallest smudge.
