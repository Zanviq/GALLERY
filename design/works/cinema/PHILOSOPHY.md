# Signal Grey

A visual philosophy for the 점멸 key visual, the trailer frames, the OG card and the favicon.

**Light as the only event.** A picture in this language is a dark field in which one thing is lit.
Everything else exists only because that light reaches it: a sill catches an edge, rain becomes a
line only where it crosses a lamp, a figure is the shape the light is missing. Nothing is outlined.
Form is found by subtracting dark, never by drawing contours, and every frame is composed so that the
eye arrives at the lit thing first and the rest of the picture is discovered slowly after it, the way
a room reveals itself while the eyes adjust. Each of these frames should feel meticulously built, the
work of someone who has stood in a dark street long enough to know exactly how far a lamp reaches.

**Grey is a scale, not a mood.** The palette is a measured ladder from black to white, chosen step
by step the way a printer chooses paper grade. Blacks are true and deep and kept for the large
areas; whites are rare and small, so that when one appears it carries the weight of the whole frame.
Middle greys do the quiet work of air, wet asphalt and plaster. There is no tint anywhere. The one
permitted colour is a voice: yellow appears only as a spoken line laid over a picture, set low and
centred as a subtitle would be, and never as decoration, accent or mark.

**The material is film.** Pictures live inside frames and frames live on a strip with numbered
edges and perforations. Grain belongs to the emulsion, so it lives only inside the image area and
never on the page around it. Halation belongs to bright points, so light bleeds softly outward from
lamps, windows and headlights and nowhere else. The rebate, the edge print, the slightly rounded
corners of the gate, the fogged end of the roll: these are rendered with painstaking fidelity,
because their accuracy is what makes drawn shapes read as photographed ones.

**Repetition as time.** A contact sheet is a calendar of seconds. The same composition repeated
three times with one small change (a bulb switched on, a headlight grown larger, a hand moved closer
to a switch) is how this language tells time. Rhythm comes from the strip: equal cells, equal gaps,
small differences between neighbours. The composition rewards the patient eye that compares frame to
frame, and every variation is deliberate, calibrated with the care of master-level craft.

**The hand on top of the machine.** Over the mechanical regularity of film sits one human trace:
the white wax pencil. Circles, an X, a short note, drawn quickly and a little unevenly, with the
waxy break of a real pencil on glossy paper. It is the only gesture in the work that is not ruled
by a grid, and it is placed sparingly so that each mark reads as a decision someone made.

**Text whispers.** Typography appears as a title card on black, as edge print in small grey
figures, as a bus-stop sign inside a picture. The title is set large only once. Everything else is
small, exact and placed so that nothing overlaps and nothing crowds the edge: the result should look
like it took countless hours, by someone at the very top of their field, and it should never ask to
be noticed before the light does.

## Applied

- Key visual (hero, OG): a building wall at night in rain; one window in the upper right, the only
  white in the picture, with halation. The title stands vertical at the opposite edge; a single
  subtitle line sits under the picture in yellow.
- Favicon: black square, one small lit window (white pane with a grey sash cross), no yellow.
- Wax pencil marks on the sheet: white, stroke drawn as two slightly offset passes with gaps.
- Revision 2026-11-09: the key visual and the frames are now generated black-and-white photographs
  (see IMAGES.md), not drawings. The same rules hold for choosing, cropping and grading them: one lit
  thing per frame, true blacks, grain only inside the picture, light changed by a cut between a lit and
  a darkened print of the same frame.
