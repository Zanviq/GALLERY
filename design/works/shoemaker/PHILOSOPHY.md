# Chalk Before Knife

A visual philosophy for 골과 본 (key visual, favicon, the drawings on the page).

Every mark is a measurement that has not been cut yet. The picture lives in the moment before the knife:
lines laid down by hand on two surfaces, a cool grey card and a dark red skin, and nothing else. Form is
the outline of something absent (a foot, a last, a piece of upper) and space is the material around it
that will become waste or will become a shoe. The composition is always a layout decision: where a
shape sits on a skin, how close the next shape may come, which way the grain runs.

Colour is material, never mood. Oxblood is the skin and the polish worked into it, two depths of one
red, flat, unlit. Chalk grey is the pattern card. White is chalk, and chalk is the only light in the
work: it is dry, slightly broken at the edges, heavier where the hand paused. There are no gradients,
no glows and no shadows; depth comes only from one surface lying on another.

Scale and rhythm come from the tools. Ruler ticks every 5 mm and every 10 mm, stitch dashes at a fixed
pitch, notches where two pieces must meet, eyelets in a strict row. Repetition is earned by function:
a mark repeats because a seam or a scale requires it, and its spacing is exact. The eye should be able
to read sizes off the picture the way a maker reads a pattern, and the work should look as if someone
spent countless patient hours getting each tick, notch and curve right.

Balance is the balance of a good cutting layout: pieces nested close but never touching, the largest
along the backbone line where the skin is best, smaller pieces in the gaps, every grain arrow parallel.
Text is a pencil note on a pattern: a piece name, a count, a millimetre figure, set small in a plain
gothic, placed inside or beside the shape it names and never across a line. One word may be large
when it is the name of the house, and then it is set in a calm brush-built serif and left alone.

The finished artefact should feel like a page from a master's working file: meticulously drawn,
quietly dense, the product of deep expertise rather than decoration. If a line does not measure,
mark, join or name something, it is removed. What stays is drawn with painstaking care, line by line,
until the skin and the card look exactly as a maker would leave them at the end of a long day.
