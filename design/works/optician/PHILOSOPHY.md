# Graded Clarity

A visual philosophy for things that are read at decreasing size, under controlled light.

**Scale is the only hierarchy.** A composition in this manner is ordered the way a wall chart is
ordered: one mark so large it cannot be missed, then rows that step down by a fixed ratio until
the last row asks for full attention. Nothing is emphasised by colour, weight tricks or ornament;
importance is simply size, and size is always a number that could be measured. Every step of the
ratio is computed, never eyeballed, and the work should feel like the patient, meticulously
crafted output of someone who has drawn ten thousand of these rows and still checks each one.

**Black on lit white, and two lamps.** The field is the white of a backlit panel, not paper. The
marks are a single near-black. The only other colours are a red and a green taken from a test
slide, and they never decorate: they appear as two equal fields side by side, a comparison the
eye is asked to judge. When they appear, they are flat, saturated, and exact, and they are
always named by position as well, because the judgment belongs to sharpness, not to hue.

**Letters built, not typed.** The characters at the heart of the work are constructed on a grid
whose stroke is one fifth of the letter's height, so every gap equals every stroke. They are
architecture more than writing: blocky, open, slightly strange, entirely legible. Their spacing
equals their width. Painstaking attention goes into keeping every counter open at every size.

**Focus as the single event.** Blur is permitted only inside a lens, and only as the subject being
measured. Around it, everything else stays perfectly crisp, so the soft region reads as optics
and not as atmosphere. The work is at its best at the moment just before full sharpness, when the
large rows have resolved and the small ones are still arriving.

**Instruments drawn as diagrams.** Wheels, frames, faces and blanks are drawn in a single line
weight with dimension ticks, the way a technician's sheet would draw them: no shading, no depth,
only outline and measure. Text is sparse and clinical, a number and a unit, a word naming a side.
The finished piece should look like master-level drafting that took countless careful hours, an
artifact from a discipline where a quarter of a dioptre is a difference worth drawing.
