# Tile and Porthole

*A visual philosophy for the 뽀득 key visual, favicon and OG image.*

A wall of small square tiles is a grid nobody designed to be looked at, and that is its
dignity. Everything in this movement stands on that grid: forms are placed on the grout
lines, never between them, so the composition reads as something fixed to a wall rather
than floated on a screen. The tile is pale mint, the grout a breath lighter, and the
whole field is laid with the patience of a tiler who checks every joint with a level.

Against the wall stand round things. The porthole is the only curve the room allows: a
thick stainless ring, a graphite gasket, a dark glass, and behind it whatever is turning.
Circles repeat in strict rows like machines in a line, identical in size and spacing, so
that the one difference between them (what is inside, which light is lit) becomes the whole
message. Repetition carries the meaning; variation is rationed to a single place.

Colour is a material, not a mood. Mint is ceramic, grey is brushed steel, graphite is the
plastic bezel around a display. Two lights exist, green and red, and they are spent only to
say whether a machine is free or busy; a light used anywhere else would be a lie. The
seven-segment numeral is the only typography the machine itself owns, and it is drawn
segment by segment, each bar bevelled, each unlit bar faintly visible behind the lit ones.

Text is a sticker, not a caption. A number cut from vinyl on a steel door, a word in rounded
letters on the wall sign. Nothing explains; the image works the way the room works, glanced at
from the doorway with a bag in one hand.

Every joint, gap and bevel must look labored over: rings concentric to the pixel, grout lines
continuous across the frame, highlights placed where one real light source would put them.
The result should feel meticulously crafted by someone who has spent many nights in a coin
laundry and many years at a drawing table, an artifact of painstaking attention to a room
most people only wait in.
