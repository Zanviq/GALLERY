# Swell Notation

*A visual philosophy for the 너울 identity. Written for canvas-design; the live site and the key visuals both follow it.*

Swell Notation treats letters as water that has learned to keep time. A word is not a fixed object set once and left alone; it is a surface under pressure, and pressure shows itself as weight and width. Where energy arrives, strokes thicken and counters close; where it leaves, the letter thins and stretches like the long, flat back of a wave far from shore. Every composition is one held instant of that rise and fall, recorded with the cold precision of an instrument reading a sea. The work must look meticulously crafted, every measurement deliberate, the product of painstaking attention by someone who has spent years setting type by hand.

Space is organized like reclaimed land: flat rectangular fields divided by low walls, each field holding a single tone. Composition is horizontal and stratified. Bands of type run edge to edge, never centered for comfort, always justified to the full measure so that each line becomes a wall of the field. The margins are the shoreline: generous where the eye needs to rest, razor-thin where the mass of a line should feel like it might spill. Balance comes from weight distribution, not symmetry; one enormous line can hold down an entire sheet the way a tide holds down a coast.

Color is printed, not lit. Each sheet carries one field color and one ink, plus a single deeper tone of the field for the drawn marks beneath the type: tide lines, a solar disc, the faint grid of evaporation pans. No gradients, no glow, no transparency used as atmosphere. Tone-on-tone layering replaces shading, so the surface reads like two passes through a stencil duplicator, registered with master-level care. Three fields exist in the system, red earth, deep sea and dried flower yellow, and each owns a day; the ink never changes its role.

Scale and rhythm come from repetition with measured deviation. A tide line is drawn many times, each pass slightly offset in phase, until the accumulation suggests a surface rather than a stroke. Type sizes step down in a strict ladder, and within each rung the width axis absorbs every difference in length so that the edges stay perfectly true. The eye should be able to read the rhythm before it reads a single word: heavy, heavy, lighter, lighter, a crest, a trough.

Text is sparse and does the heavy lifting only as form. Names and numbers are the only words that appear at scale; everything else is a small, plain label placed like a surveyor's note at the edge of the map. Nothing overlaps unless the overlap is the point, nothing falls off the sheet, and every alignment has been checked again and again. The finished piece should feel like a scientific chart of something that cannot be measured, rendered with the composure and craftsmanship of a master at the absolute top of the field.
