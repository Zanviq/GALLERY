# Cast Drafting

A movement for drawings that measure what cannot be held. One hue, the deep blue of a cyanotype, and
the white of the line that is scratched into it. Every form exists twice: once as the thing, drawn thin
and exact, and once as what the thing does to light, laid down as solid colour on paper. The pleasure is
the distance between the two, and the honesty of the construction lines that connect them.

Space is orthographic and obedient. Views stack on a shared axis, so a point above is the same point
below; a reader can drop a ruler through the sheet and every edge lines up. Nothing floats for effect.
Margins are wide and even, the sheet has a border, and the border has a reason. The composition is
balanced the way an engineering drawing is balanced: by the needs of the views, never by decoration.

Colour is a single pigment at different strengths. The ground is full strength; construction is a pale
wash of it; the paper screen is the absence of it; the cast form returns to full strength, so the
shadow and the sheet are literally the same blue. Softness is never blurred. Where an edge softens, it
softens in visible steps, like a stack of thin papers slightly offset, each one placed by hand. That
stepped penumbra is the signature, and it must look meticulously counted, not filtered.

Line carries the hierarchy. Primary edges are firm; hidden edges are dashed in a quiet rhythm; rays leave
the light in thin, straight, unhurried lines and stop exactly where they land. Hatching appears only on
what is cut, at one angle, at one pitch. Every stroke weight is chosen once and kept, the product of
painstaking calibration rather than a default.

Text is a pencil note in the margin: a few handwritten words, a number with its unit, a title in a
single stencilled face. It labels; it never explains. Scale is the drama: small, careful marks around
one large plane of white where a single dark silhouette has become far larger than the hand that cut it.

The finished sheet should look as though a master draughtsman spent weeks on it, checking every
tangent, every vanishing ray, every offset copy of an edge: crisp, quiet, exact, and slightly in awe of
something as ordinary as a shadow.
